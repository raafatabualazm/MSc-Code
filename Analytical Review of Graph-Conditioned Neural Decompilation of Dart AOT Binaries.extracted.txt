Analytical Review of Graph-Conditioned Neural
Decompilation of Dart AOT Binaries
Executive summary
This manuscript is best understood as an empirical machine-learning systems paper aimed at
readers in reverse engineering, program analysis, neural code generation, and software engineering
research. Its present structure and numbered citation style make it look far closer to an IEEE/ACM-style
technical paper than to an APA-style manuscript. If the intended venue is double-blind, however, the
current title page and self-citations would need immediate anonymisation; if the intended venue is a
journal with a house style, that guide should take precedence over any generic convention.
fileciteturn0file0 1
Overall, the paper has a promising core contribution: it identifies a genuinely important phenomenon
in neural decompilation, namely that better surface metrics do not necessarily yield better functional
correctness, and it presents a useful empirical contrast between graph conditioning, reward adaptation,
reranking, and multi-arm candidate pooling. The paper is also sensibly organised at a high level, with
Introduction, Related Work, Method, Experimental Setup, Results, Discussion, Threats to Validity, Future
Work, and Conclusion. fileciteturn0file0
My overall recommendation is major revision. The key reasons are not that the paper lacks merit, but
that several issues currently weaken its scientific reliability and publication readiness:

Suggested abstract and title options
Rewritten abstract
Neural decompilation is often evaluated with surface-form and compilability metrics, yet these
measures can diverge from functional correctness. We study whether graph-conditioned decoding and
inference-time candidate selection improve neural decompilation of Dart AOT binaries beyond
sequence-only fine-tuning. Our approach encodes control-flow and lightweight data-flow structure
extracted from x86-64 Dart AOT assembly using a GraphCodeBERT-based encoder and conditions a
Qwen3-8B decoder through learned graph-prefix representations. We evaluate architecture ablations,
GRPO-based reward adaptation, rejection-sampling supervised fine-tuning, compile-aware reranking,
and multi-arm candidate pooling on 154 HumanEval-Dart tasks, with separate denominators for
compile-oriented and pass-oriented evaluations. The clean graph-conditioned model provides the
strongest overall balance of CodeBLEU, compilability, and functional correctness, reaching 0.638
CodeBLEU, compile@5 of 0.667, and pass@10 of 0.156. A binary-reward GRPO variant attains the
highest standalone pass@10 of 0.182, but at substantial cost to compile@5 and CodeBLEU, indicating
instability under sparse correctness rewards. No single adaptation arm dominates across all metrics.
Instead, pooled candidate sets reveal substantial complementarity across arms, yielding at least one
passing candidate for 43 of 154 tasks. We therefore conclude that graph conditioning improves the
most balanced single-model profile, whereas multi-arm candidate generation and stronger selection
mechanisms are the most promising route to higher functional recovery in Dart AOT decompilation.
