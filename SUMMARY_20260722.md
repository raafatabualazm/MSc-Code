## 2. VeRPO V1 (reward-based GRPO) — attempted, blocked on OOM

**Design:** GRPO from the 08b checkpoint, reward = `shaped_compiler + 1.5·judge` (compile-gated). The judge (**deepseek-v4-pro**, temp 0, `max_tokens=16000`) scores *functional progress vs the tests* — never reference similarity (avoids the TOSEM metric-divergence trap) — creating within-group reward variance exactly where the compiler reward is flat (compile@10 98.6% / pass@10 0.36% ⇒ every sample compiles-and-fails ⇒ zero gradient without the judge). Judge validated (`deepseek-v4-pro` resolves; auth via `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY`).

**Blocked:** 7 launches, all OOM at batch 4 (~94 GiB / 95 GiB card). Levers tried and ruled out:
- group_size 8→4→2 (advanced step 2→4, never cleared).
- `GRPO_SCORE_CHUNK_SIZE=1` (per-candidate scoring backward).
- `grpo_grad_accum=1` (no cross-batch retention).
- `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True`.
- `--long_prompt_max_tokens 8192` (down from 12,288) — **did not help**, which localizes the wall to the **graph encoder on the single long ≥200 row** (its cost scales with CFG block count, hundreds of blocks, ~25–30 GiB per the code's own comment — independent of prompt length). The encoder is **inert** for the text arm (frozen `no_grad`, contributes nothing) yet still consumes the memory. GRPO has **no built-in graph-skip flag**.
- Persistent footprint confirmed ~20 GiB ⇒ LoRA-only (optimizer is not the hog); it's activation on the long row.

**Conclusion:** not a config problem. Needs either (A) a code change to skip the inert graph encoder in the GRPO forward for the text arm, (B) filtering ≥200 rows out of GRPO (loses the cliff), or (C) a larger-memory GPU. **Recommend (A) done fresh next session.** The pod SSH also degraded badly (~04:15Z+), cutting commands and spawning zombie processes, compounding the debugging.

## 6. Theoretical synthesis

The decisive pairing established today: **the functional-repair signal demonstrably exists** (a frontier model finds it for 152/338 tasks incl. 31 on the cliff), **but plain distillation cannot transfer it to the 8B** (RS-SFT = +0.0 pp). Imitation of verified answers is not the lever — the student cannot learn the answer by copying it. That is exactly the RL-with-verifiable-feedback regime: the student must *generate*, see *why* it failed (compiler + judge), and be rewarded for *functional progress*. VeRPO is the remaining untested lever, and the OOM wall is an infrastructure obstacle to testing it — not a refutation of it.
